# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

OctoAcme uses an iterative project management lifecycle that moves work from initiation and planning through execution, release, and retrospective improvement. Projects begin with a lightweight one-pager that defines the problem, goal, success metrics, stakeholders, timeline, early risks, and resource needs. Once stakeholders align on priority, success measures, and team availability, the team moves into planning by building a prioritized backlog, defining acceptance criteria and the Definition of Done, identifying dependencies, and mapping milestones to a release plan.

During execution, teams track work on a project board and use small pull requests, peer review, and CI checks to keep delivery moving in testable increments. Risks and dependencies are maintained in a risk register, reviewed in regular syncs, and escalated through the documented team, PM, Product Lead, and sponsor path when needed. Release readiness is based on completed acceptance criteria, merged pull requests, passing CI and security checks, prepared smoke tests, release notes, rollback planning, post-deployment verification, and release communication to stakeholders and support.

The core personas in this process are Project Managers, who coordinate plans, schedules, risks, dependencies, and communications; Product Managers, who define outcomes and prioritize the backlog; Developers, who build, test, and review the solution; QA/Testing contributors, who validate quality and acceptance criteria; and stakeholders, who provide input, decisions, and approvals. Communication relies on standups, weekly delivery and PM syncs, milestone demos or reviews, regular stakeholder updates, a single source of truth such as the project README or release doc, risk-register reviews, and clear escalation paths. Quality assurance is continuous and includes acceptance criteria, Definition of Done, unit, integration, and end-to-end testing as applicable, CI tests and linting, security scanning, manual QA when needed, smoke tests, and retrospective follow-through for continuous improvement.
